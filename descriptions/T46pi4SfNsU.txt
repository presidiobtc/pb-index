Modern AI systems are often cast as products: a model, a chatbot, an API. But for science, that framing is too narrow. Scientific communities need more than access to finished systems: they need the ability to understand, modify, reproduce, and extend them. That requires open AI infrastructure spanning the model-development lifecycle, from data and training methods to intermediate artifacts, evaluations, and documentation. In this talk, I will use Ai2’s Olmo project as a case study, highlighting examples of what becomes possible when researchers can study and intervene in model construction rather than only consume its outputs. I will argue that this kind of openness is essential if universities, nonprofits, governments, and scientific communities are to build AI systems that serve their own purposes. The goal is not to reproduce commercial AI in the open, but to create an ecosystem that gives scientific communities greater agency and accelerates discovery.
